1. **A student can earn every star without evaluating a climate impact.** Picking infrared, spotting a hot year and issuing a warning demonstrates instrument recall and anomaly detection. The missing step is deciding how strongly observations support an impact claim. Make the core question “How well did accumulated heat stress explain the pattern of GBR bleaching in 2024?” Have students compare two reefs, inspect several summers against the same seasonal baseline, then reveal independent aerial surveys. Require one quantified comparison and one limit on attribution. Assess the collection half separately by asking why a reef had no usable observation on a particular pass. [NSW syllabus](https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/learning-areas/science/science-7-10-2023/content) · [AIMS 2024 bleaching surveys](https://www.aims.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-04/FINAL-Aerial%20Bleaching%20GBR2024Report_AIMS_Final_15Apr2024_0.pdf)

2. **The globe animation risks teaching the wrong physics.** A universal sweep that fills every pixel erases the distinction between a measurement and an estimate. Geostationary satellites repeatedly view a fixed region, with poor high-latitude coverage. Reference radar altimeters sample narrow ground tracks. Thermal infrared senses radiation from the ocean skin through clear sky. NOAA’s CoralTemp is already a blended, gap-filled nighttime surface-temperature product. Show one real footprint, one cloud gap and its quality flag before revealing the composite. Compare with a buoy at a labelled depth. Use Degree Heating Weeks for accumulated bleaching stress and surveys for observed bleaching. [NOAA CoralTemp methodology](https://coralreefwatch.noaa.gov/product/5km/methodology.php) · [NOAA altimetry tracks](https://www.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/socd/lsa/SeaLevelRise/LSA_SLR_timeseries.php)

3. **Four build–fly–analyse–brief cycles crowd out the reasoning.** After five minutes to settle and five for an exit ticket, each case gets ten minutes including instructions and transitions. Budget 5 minutes for launch, 8 for all four sensor–variable pairings, 20 for the reef investigation, 10 for three short transfer cases and 7 for an individual exit ticket. Cut spacecraft shopping, repeated flights and the compulsory 3D globe. Pilot on a managed Chromebook over school Wi-Fi. Provide a keyboard-operable 2D map, labelled patterns, graph data tables and untimed responses.

4. **The sea-level case conflates a global average with island flood exposure.** TOPEX/Poseidon launched in 1992. The standard 1993-onward record continues through Jason-1/2/3 and Sentinel-6. Radar pulse travel time gives range; precise orbital height and corrections yield sea-surface height. Roughly 3–4 mm/year is defensible as a satellite-era global average, but WMO reports 2.1 for 1993–2002 and 4.7 for 2015–2024. Compare the global series with a local tide gauge measuring water relative to land. Frame the island question around a published Torres Strait Islander priority and seek permission for community observations. [NASA record](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5516/) · [WMO rates](https://wmo.int/sites/default/files/2025-03/WMO-1368-2024_en.pdf) · [TSRA Rangers](https://www.tsra.gov.au/the-tsra/programs/environmental-management/rangers/)

5. **The forest and ice mysteries invite false causal verdicts.** Landsat measures reflected light. Hansen classifies annual tree-cover loss at 30 m; a loss pixel alone cannot distinguish fire, harvest or Queensland clearing. Compare a mapped fire perimeter with a verified clearing polygon. Black Summer attribution research concerns increased *fire-weather risk*, not the cause of each ignition. For Antarctica, passive microwave emissions estimate ice concentration; extent counts ocean cells with at least 15% ice. Both the February minimum and September maximum set record lows in 2023. Compare like seasons across decades. Neither extreme measures ice-sheet mass or establishes a trend alone. [Hansen product](https://storage.googleapis.com/earthenginepartners-hansen/GFC-2024-v1.12/download.html) · [Black Summer attribution study](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/21/941/2021/index.html) · [NSIDC sea-ice methods](https://nsidc.org/sea-ice-today/about-data)

6. **The winning strategy is to guess, retry and always warn.** Stars decorate a worksheet if every brief has one correct sensor and warnings cost nothing. Give the player one survey flight and two reefs with conflicting evidence. Choosing where to send it should change what they learn. Auto-score structured evidence selections, numerical comparisons with tolerances, and recognition of insufficient evidence. Leave the minister’s free-text reasoning to a teacher rubric. A keyword matcher can reward “warming explains everything.”

7. **“Open data” is too vague to build from.** NOAA permits Coral Reef Watch redistribution. Hansen v1.12 permits reuse under CC BY 4.0 with attribution and changes identified. NASA offers a downloadable sea-level text series and defaults NASA-led data to CC0 unless marked otherwise. NSIDC publishes Sea Ice Index v4 tables; I could verify availability but not an explicit product-specific redistribution licence. Resolve that before bundling. Precompute dated CSV extracts and cropped map tiles. Set a core download target of 5 MB and measure it on-device. 8. **The standalone decision rests partly on a mistaken inventory.** Carrying Capacity already sells ground-station subscriptions for SST, sea level, forest and **fire**. Ice is absent. Its sea-level blurb also implies altimetry identifies which streets flood first. Give the investigation a direct classroom URL and a link from the existing drawer. Correct that blurb and label the town’s simulated overlays distinctly from measured data.
